data_scripts / form13f

The form13f dataset

Quarterly SEC Form 13F institutional holdings, one parquet per quarter-end report period — and a field guide to its sharpest edge: what manager_name does and does not tell you, and how to read the other_manager* columns.

53 quarters, 20130331 → 20260331 ~3.0–3.2M rows / recent quarter ~8,700 filers / recent quarter files: data/YYYYMMDD.parquet builder: save_form13f.py

If you read nothing else

  1. Identity is cik, never manager_name. The name is free-text from the cover page: 1,757 of 13,076 all-time CIKs (13.4%) have two or more spellings across quarters. Cast cik to integer before comparing — the string is inconsistently zero-padded (0001067983 Berkshire vs 872786 BNP Paribas).
  2. manager_name is the filer, not necessarily the manager. One filing can cover dozens of managers. The economic managers sit in other_manager_names — 57% of 2025Q1 reported value is on rows that carry them, and the biggest real-world managers (BlackRock Fund Advisors $1.9T, Fidelity's FMR Co LLC $1.0T, Morgan Stanley Smith Barney $605B) never appear in manager_name at all.
  3. A row is one block of shares, not one position. The same filer legitimately reports the same CUSIP on many rows (BlackRock: AAPL on 24 rows, one per subsidiary, 1.14B shares total; Berkshire: 12 rows summing to exactly 300,000,000). Sum rows to get positions.
  4. other_manager_names is the complete resolved list; other_manager_ciks is a subset. Only 64% of included-manager entries carry a CIK — never zip the two columns positionally, and match managers by CIK or name.
  5. value switches units. Officially $1,000s through period 20220930 (though ~14% of filers reported plain dollars anyway), a 95/5 mix at 20221231, clean USD from 20230331 on. Normalize per filer, not per quarter.
  6. Cross-filing double counting is rare but real. The included-manager / notice-filing system is designed to report each block once, and mostly does (Millennium ∩ WorldQuant: zero overlapping blocks). The residue concentrates in OTR rows and duplicate registrations — $1.4bn of matched duplicate blocks out of $53T in 2025Q1.

§1What a 13F filing is

An institutional investment manager — any entity, or natural person, exercising investment discretion over $100M+ of “13(f) securities” — must report those holdings to the SEC within 45 days of each calendar quarter end. 13(f) securities are the exchange-traded universe on the SEC’s official list: US-listed stocks and ETFs, certain convertibles, and exchange-traded options. Long positions only — no shorts, no bonds outside the list, no cash, no non-US listings.

A filing has two parts that matter here:

  • a cover page: the filing manager’s name and CIK, the report type, amendment info, and a numbered “List of Other Included Managers” — managers whose holdings are contained in this filing;
  • an information table: one row per block of shares, with issuer, CUSIP, value, share count, put/call flag, investment discretion, voting authority — and column 7, which lists the sequence numbers of the included managers that share discretion over that row.

Three report types exist. 13F HOLDINGS REPORT: all of the filer’s reportable holdings are in this filing. 13F COMBINATION REPORT: some holdings are here, the rest are reported inside some other manager’s filing. 13F NOTICE: none are here — everything is reported by others. This is how fund complexes avoid reporting the same block twice: one entity files an umbrella report, and the subsidiaries file notices (or nothing).

§2How the dataset is built

save_form13f.py consumes the SEC Form 13F structured data sets (TSV dumps of every XML filing) rather than crawling EDGAR:

  1. Window → period. The SEC packages filings by receipt window, not report period. The script picks the window containing the period’s due date, then keeps only PERIODOFREPORT == period (~92% of a window’s submissions; the rest are stragglers for older periods). Filings for a period that arrive after its window closes are therefore absent.
  2. Submission filter. Keeps 13F-HR and 13F-HR/A only. 13F-NT notices carry no holdings and are dropped — a notice-only manager never appears as a filer (§4, G4).
  3. Amendment de-duplication. Per CIK: the last original-or-RESTATEMENT filing wins; later NEW HOLDINGS amendments are added on top (§8).
  4. Merge. INFOTABLE ⟕ SUBMISSION ⟕ COVERPAGE on accession number; the OTHERMANAGER2 lookup resolves column-7 sequence refs into other_manager_names / other_manager_ciks (§4.2).
  5. Tickers. CUSIP→symbol via OpenFIGI (US composite pass, then any-listing equities-only pass), cached in cache/cusip_ticker.parquet (§6).

Output is one zstd parquet per quarter, sorted by cik, then value descending. Known limitations, all inherited from the source or the design above: late filings outside the receipt window are missing; the cover page’s reverse manager list (OTHERMANAGER.tsv, “other managers reporting for this manager”) is not joined, so a combination filer’s file does not say who reports its remaining holdings — that information only exists in the other direction, via the umbrella filer’s other_manager* columns; figi is null before mid-2023 (the SEC added the column then); and 20130331 has only 69 filers (XML filing was phased in during 2013 — coverage is full from 20130630).

§4The manager model — manager_name & other_manager*

This is the part of 13F data that everyone gets wrong first. All figures below are from 20250331.parquet (3,035,099 rows, 7,987 filers, $52.95T as filed) and generalize to other quarters.

4.1 One filing, many managers

Form 13F separates who files from who manages. A parent files one report; the subsidiaries, sub-advisers, trust companies — even natural persons — whose holdings it contains are enumerated on the cover page as other included managers, and each holdings row points at the ones that share investment discretion over that block:

One 13F-HR filing — accession_number 0000950123-25-…
Cover page filing manager: CIK + name → dataset columns cik, manager_name (every row)
list of other included managers (source table OTHERMANAGER2):
  seq 1 · name · 13F file number · CIK (CIK optional — often blank!)
  seq 2 · …   seq N · …
Information table — one row per block issuer · cusip · value · shares · discretion · voting · col 7: "1,5" ← sequence refs into the cover-page list
column 7 refs × OTHERMANAGER2 lookup, per accession ↓
Dataset columns, per row other_manager = "1,5" (verbatim) · other_manager_names = "Mgr A; Mgr B" · other_manager_ciks = "0001…; 0001…" (only entries that have a CIK)

Sequence numbers are meaningful only within one accession — "2" in an Alger filing is Weatherbie Capital; "2" in a State Street filing is an SSGA entity. The dataset has already done the per-accession resolution for you; the raw string is kept for audit only.

4.2 Reading the three columns

columnwhat it istrust level
other_manager Column 7 verbatim. Usually seq refs ("1", "1,5", "01"), but filers also write "0", "NONE", "N/A", free-text names, CRD numbers… Audit trail only. Never parse it yourself
other_manager_names Names of the refs that resolved against the cover-page list, ascending seq order, "; "-joined. Null when nothing resolved The complete resolved list. Split on "; "
other_manager_ciks CIKs of the resolved managers, same seq order — skipping entries whose cover-page listing had no CIK A subset of the names list. Equal split-lengths ⇒ aligned 1:1 with names; shorter ⇒ you cannot tell which CIK belongs to which name

The subset relationship is one-directional by construction: a resolved entry always has a name, not always a CIK. Empirically the CIK list is never longer than the name list (0 rows in 2025Q1), so: equal lengths → safe to zip; unequal → use names only.

4.3 investment_discretion × other managers: the four patterns

investment_discretion says who else has discretion over the row’s block, relative to this filing: SOLE — nobody outside the managers this row is filed for; DFND (“shared-defined”) — shared among managers all covered by this filing; OTR (“shared-other”) — shared with a manager that files its own 13F. Crossing that with the presence of other-manager names yields four patterns that cover the whole dataset:

patternmeaning2025Q1 valuecanonical example
SOLE, no names Filer manages the block itself, alone. The simple case $19.9T Millennium: all 5,787 rows; Vanguard’s main block ($5.2T)
SOLE + names Parent files on a subsidiary’s behalf; the named manager runs the block. The filer may have no discretion at all $5.7T BlackRock, Inc. filing one row per sub-adviser; Fred Alger filing for Weatherbie Capital
DFND + names Discretion shared among managers all inside this filing — block appears once, sharers listed $23.3T State Street: one AAPL row naming 10 SSGA entities; Berkshire naming Warren Buffett + insurance subs
OTR (± names) Shared with a manager filing separately — the same block may exist in that other filing too. The only pattern where cross-filing duplication is expected $1.28T (2.4%) Guardian Point ↔ Mirabella (regulatory-hosting platform), G8

DFND/OTR rows without resolved names (~$2.9T) are mostly the “0” / “NONE” garbage of G5, not a fifth pattern.

A name-based “Fidelity” query catches fragments of this at random.

4.5 Gotchas

G1manager_name is unstable free text

It’s whatever the filer typed on that quarter’s cover page. 1,757 of 13,076 all-time filer CIKs (13.4%) have 2+ spellings across quarters: “Blackstone Group Inc” → “Blackstone Group Inc.” → “Blackstone Inc.”; “NATIXIS ADVISORS, L.P.” → “NATIXIS ADVISORS, LLC”; rebrands like “FIRST FINANCIAL HOLDINGS” → “SOUTH STATE CORP” (6 variants on one CIK). Within a single quarter file a CIK has exactly one name — drift is across quarters.

Do group and join on cik; treat manager_name as a display label, taking the latest quarter’s spelling.

G2cik strings are inconsistently padded

Mostly 10-digit zero-padded ("0001067983"), but some filings carry raw unpadded strings ("872786" BNP PARIBAS, "1512020" Kepos Capital) — in the same column, same quarter. other_manager_ciks has the same disease.

Do pl.col("cik").cast(pl.Int64) once, immediately, on both columns. Never compare CIKs as strings.

G3CIKs aren’t eternal either

BlackRock filed as cik 1364742 (“BlackRock Inc.”) through 20240630, then re-registered and has filed as cik 2012383 (“BlackRock, Inc.”) since 20240930. A naive per-CIK time series shows the world’s largest asset manager vanishing and a $4.5T “new manager” appearing in 2024Q3.

Do maintain a small manual successor map for entities you track; detect candidates by a filer disappearing while another appears with ≈ the same name and value.

G4The most important managers never appear in manager_name

81% of included-manager CIKs (1,255 of 1,539 in 2025Q1) never file — they’re notice filers or subsidiaries. That set includes household names, with the value reported on rows naming them: BlackRock Fund Advisors ($1.89T), BlackRock Institutional Trust Co ($1.25T), Fidelity’s FMR Co LLC ($1.04T), J.P. Morgan Investment Management ($677B — named by 5 different filers), Morgan Stanley Smith Barney ($605B), Susquehanna Securities ($563B), Merrill’s Managed Account Advisors ($517B), Jane Street Options ($344B), GSAM L.P. ($309B). manager_name-only analysis is blind to all of them.

Do search both sides: cik and other_manager_ciks/other_manager_names (recipe in §4.6).

G5other_manager (raw) is dirty; unresolved ≠ none

Of 1,397,337 rows with non-null other_manager in 2025Q1, 22% resolve to nothing: "0" (219,036 rows — “no other manager” by convention; seq 0 never exists), "NONE"/"None"/"NO"/"N/A" (~40k), free-text names where numbers belong (“AQR Capital Mgmt”, “Arax Advisory Partners”, “Vanguard Just Invest”), and CRD-like junk numbers (“146898”). There are also 4,271 rows referencing seq "1" where the filer simply never supplied a cover-page list.

Do use other_manager_names is not null as the “has other managers” predicate. Null names means no resolvable other manager — usually genuinely none, occasionally a filer’s clerical failure.

G6other_manager_ciks systematically undercounts

The cover-page CIK field is optional and 35.7% of entries omit it: 1,315,107 resolved name entries vs 845,938 CIK entries in 2025Q1. 363,335 rows (33% of resolved rows) have names but zero CIKs — including every SSGA entity on every State Street row. CIK-only linkage misses entire complexes; and when the lists differ in length, positional pairing of names with CIKs is undefined.

Do treat names as the source of truth for coverage, CIKs as a precision upgrade where present; zip them only when split-lengths match.

G7Included-manager names are per-filing spellings, in sequence order

Each filer types its own version of a sub’s name, so the same entity varies across filings (J.P. Morgan Investment Management appears via 5 unrelated filers, spelled their way). Order inside other_manager_names is ascending sequence number — an arbitrary cover-page numbering, not importance (State Street’s raw refs: 10,11,12,19,2,20,299,5,6,8).

Do normalize (casefold, strip punctuation) before grouping by included-manager name; read nothing into position.

G8Cross-filing duplication: rare, real, and findable

The included-manager system mostly prevents the same block appearing twice (§4.4: Millennium ∩ WorldQuant = 0; AllianceBernstein ∩ Equitable = 0; Cubist ∩ Point72 = 3 trivial blocks). But a strict scan of 2025Q1 — rows whose single named other manager also files, with an identical (cusip, shares) block in that manager’s own filing — still finds 92 duplicated blocks, $1.4bn: hedge funds on regulatory-hosting platforms (Guardian Point reports 350,000 APO OTR naming Mirabella; Mirabella files the same block SOLE), duplicate registrations (Atlantic Union Bankshares, twice, cross-naming itself, $0.62bn), advisers reporting a client’s account the client also reports (Advisor OS ↔ MJP Associates, same 150,079 VOO). The linkage is often one-directional — B frequently doesn’t name A back.

Do nothing for market-level aggregates (noise at $53T scale) — but when computing crowding/ownership for a specific security or manager pair, dedupe identical (cusip, shares) blocks across filings linked by other_manager*, and treat OTR rows as the suspect set.

4.6 Recipes

Normalize identity once per session:

import polars as pl

df = pl.read_parquet("data/20250331.parquet").with_columns(
    cik_i=pl.col("cik").cast(pl.Int64),                      # G2
    om_ciks=pl.col("other_manager_ciks").str.split("; ")
              .list.eval(pl.element().cast(pl.Int64)),
    om_names=pl.col("other_manager_names").str.split("; "),
)

Everything a manager touches — as filer or included manager (G4). CIK linkage is precise; add a name pattern when the target’s subsidiaries file without CIKs (G6):

MILLENNIUM = 1273087
linked = df.filter(
    (pl.col("cik_i") == MILLENNIUM)
    | pl.col("om_ciks").list.contains(MILLENNIUM)
    | pl.col("other_manager_names").str.contains("(?i)millennium management")
)
# Millennium 2025Q1: own filing $187.9bn + WorldQuant sleeve $15.9bn, 0 overlap

One security through one included manager — in cyc, sym plus an other_manager_names filter:

df.s('BABA', f=col('other_manager_names').str.contains('Jane Street Europe'))._A
# BABA rows where Jane Street Europe is among the included managers — two hits all-time,
# both in Jane Street Group's filing: 2021Q4 (10,809 sh) and 2026Q1 (16,567 sh)

A manager’s position in one security — sum the rows, keep options and principal out:

brk_aapl = df.filter(
    (pl.col("cik_i") == 1067983) & (pl.col("cusip") == "037833100")
    & pl.col("put_call").is_null() & (pl.col("shares_type") == "SH")
).select(pl.col("shares").sum(), pl.col("value").sum())
# 12 rows -> exactly 300,000,000 shares

Explode included managers safely — names are the full list; attach CIKs only when aligned (G6):

om = (df.filter(pl.col("om_names").is_not_null())
        .with_columns(aligned=pl.col("om_names").list.len()
                      == pl.col("om_ciks").list.len().fill_null(0))
        .explode("om_names"))
# group_by("om_names") ≈ per-included-manager attribution (normalize spellings first, G7)

Duplicate-block sweep for a security/pair study (G8):

own = df.select("cik_i", "cusip", "shares")
suspect = (df.filter(pl.col("om_ciks").list.len() == 1)
             .with_columns(om=pl.col("om_ciks").list.first())
             .join(own, left_on=["om", "cusip", "shares"],
                   right_on=["cik_i", "cusip", "shares"], how="semi"))
# rows whose named manager's own filing repeats the identical block

§5value units and the 2023 break

Form 13F required values rounded to the nearest $1,000 until the SEC’s electronic-filing modernization took effect on 2023-01-03; filings from then on use whole dollars. In receipt terms: periods ≤ 20220930 are officially thousands, period 20221231 (filed Jan–Feb 2023) is the transition, and periods ≥ 20230331 are dollars. Reality is messier — compliance with the old convention was never universal. Bucketing each filer by the ratio of its own totals across the break:

periodfiled in $1,000sfiled in dollarsambiguous
20220930 (official: thousands)85.5%14.3%0.2%
20221231 (transition)5.2%94.6%0.1%
≥ 20230331—100%—

So pre-2023 quarter-level value sums are meaningless mixtures (a seventh of filers inflate the sum 1000×), and any per-manager series crossing 2023 has a step. Berkshire’s biggest AAPL row, same 669,429,166 shares in consecutive quarters:

periodsharesvalue as filedmeaning
20220630669,429,16691,524,356$1,000s → $91.5bn
20221231669,429,16686,841,985,318dollars → $86.8bn

Per-filer normalization: compare each filer’s pre-2023 total against its own total in a clean post-2023 quarter; a ratio near 1000 marks a thousands-filer. Empirically only ~0.2% of filers land in the ambiguous middle:

ref = (pl.read_parquet("data/20230630.parquet", columns=["cik", "value"])
         .group_by(pl.col("cik").cast(pl.Int64)).agg(ref=pl.col("value").sum()))
q = pl.read_parquet("data/20220630.parquet").with_columns(cik_i=pl.col("cik").cast(pl.Int64))
kilo = (q.group_by("cik_i").agg(v=pl.col("value").sum())
          .join(ref, left_on="cik_i", right_on="cik")
          .filter(pl.col("ref") / pl.col("v") > 100)["cik_i"])   # thousands-filers
q = q.with_columns(value_usd=pl.when(pl.col("cik_i").is_in(kilo))
                     .then(pl.col("value") * 1000).otherwise(pl.col("value")))
# filers absent from the reference quarter need a price sanity check instead

§6Securities, tickers, options

  • cusip is the reliable security key (uppercased, 9 chars). issuer_name/title_of_class are filer-typed free text.
  • figi is only populated from mid-2023 (SEC schema addition), and only when the filer supplied it.
  • Symbols are OpenFIGI-derived at build time, not as-of the period. sym is the instrument’s own FIGI ticker (bond descriptors for debt); sym_und is the equity underlier — equal to sym for equities, parsed from the descriptor for Corp/Pfd (BABA 0.5 06/01/31 → BABA), null for Govt/Muni/Mtge and unmapped CUSIPs. Delisted/renamed securities may be null or carry today’s symbol, not the historical one.
  • Options (~4% of rows: 60,279 Put / 61,179 Call in 2025Q1) are reported as underlying-share equivalents: shares and value describe the underlying shares, flagged by put_call. Include them and you overstate economic exposure; share-ownership studies should filter put_call.is_null().
  • shares_type == "PRN" (16,645 rows) means shares is a principal amount (convertibles etc.), not a count — filter to "SH" when summing share quantities.

§8Amendments and filing multiplicity

The builder already resolved amendments — per (period, CIK), the last original-or-RESTATEMENT filing replaces everything before it, then NEW HOLDINGS amendments (the mechanism for adding omitted positions, including expired confidential treatment) stack on top. What remains visible in the data, 2025Q1:

Two traps follow. is_amendment is tri-state: 4,015 filers have it null, so filter(~pl.col("is_amendment")) silently drops 60% of the dataset — write is_amendment.fill_null(False) or filter on amendment_type. And a filer can span multiple accessions in one period (13 filers in 2025Q1, base + NEW HOLDINGS): “one filer, one filing” is almost always true, not always. accession_number is the real filing key; (cik, cusip) is not unique; (accession_number, row) is.
